title: "Poor night vision: causes, and what the research shows"
---
<p>By Dr. Glen Swartwout, Naturopathic Optometrist</p>
<p>I am retired from licensed practice, and I now write and teach about natural eye care.</p>
<p>This page is education, not a diagnosis or a treatment plan.</p>
<p>Many people find it harder to see in dim light as they age.</p>
<p>Night driving, dark rooms and the walk from a bright shop to a dark parking lot can all get harder.</p>
<p>Poor night vision is a symptom, and it has many possible causes.</p>
<p>Some are harmless, and some need an eye doctor.</p>
<h2>How night vision works</h2>
<p>Rod cells in the retina handle dim light.</p>
<p>They use a light-sensing pigment that must be rebuilt after bright light.</p>
<p>That rebuilding needs a form of vitamin A.</p>
<p>One review says full recovery "takes tens of minutes to return completely to normal."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15177205/">Dark adaptation and the retinoid cycle of vision</a></p>
<p>This recovery slows with age.</p>
<p>In a study of 94 adults, recovery time "increased 2.76 min/decade."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10748929/">Aging and dark adaptation</a></p>
<h2>Common causes</h2>
<h3>Cataract</h3>
<p>A clouding lens scatters light from headlights and street lamps.</p>
<p>In one study, older drivers with cataract who chose surgery were compared with those who did not.</p>
<p>Those who had surgery had about half the crash rate, a rate ratio of "0.47."</p>
<p>This was an observational study, which cannot prove cause.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12186601/">Impact of cataract surgery on motor vehicle crash involvement by older adults</a></p>
<p>Read more on my <a href="https://courses.mentorshipu.com/learn/natural-eye-care/01-conditions/cataract">cataract page</a>.</p>
<h3>Night myopia</h3>
<p>Some eyes become slightly nearsighted in the dark.</p>
<p>In a study of 136 professional drivers, "Night myopia was found in 34 drivers (25%)."</p>
<p>Young drivers with more of it had more night-time accidents.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17335516/">Relationship between night myopia and night-time motor vehicle accidents</a></p>
<p>A 2026 study of 115 people found no link between night myopia and reported driving trouble.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41642820/">Self-perceived far vision quality and night driving difficulties are not related to night myopia or visual acuity</a></p>
<h3>Early macular degeneration</h3>
<p>Slow recovery on a lab test can come before macular degeneration shows on an exam.</p>
<p>In one study, eyes with slow recovery were "approximately 2 times more likely to have AMD" three years later.</p>
<p>AMD stands for age-related macular degeneration.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26522707/">Delayed Rod-Mediated Dark Adaptation Is a Functional Biomarker for Incident Early Age-Related Macular Degeneration</a></p>
<p>Read more on my <a href="https://courses.mentorshipu.com/learn/natural-eye-care/01-conditions/macular-degeneration">macular degeneration page</a>.</p>
<h3>Retinitis pigmentosa</h3>
<p>In this inherited disease, people "typically lose night vision in adolescence."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17113430/">Retinitis pigmentosa</a></p>
<p>Read more on my <a href="https://courses.mentorshipu.com/learn/natural-eye-care/01-conditions/retinitis-pigmentosa">retinitis pigmentosa page</a>.</p>
<h3>Diabetes</h3>
<p>In a small study of 24 people with type 2 diabetes, some had delayed dark adaptation.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20732318/">Dark adaptation during transient hyperglycemia in type 2 diabetes</a></p>
<p>Read more on my <a href="https://courses.mentorshipu.com/learn/natural-eye-care/01-conditions/diabetic-retinopathy">diabetic retinopathy page</a>.</p>
<h3>Glaucoma</h3>
<p>Glaucoma often affects side vision first.</p>
<p>In one study, field loss in the lower half of vision was linked with trouble in low light.</p>
<p>The authors noted this was "especially with driving."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41544815/">Association between Vision-Related Difficulties in Low Light Levels and Visual Fields in Glaucoma</a></p>
<p>Read more on my <a href="https://courses.mentorshipu.com/learn/natural-eye-care/01-conditions/glaucoma">glaucoma page</a>.</p>
<h3>Dry eye</h3>
<p>An unstable tear film can blur vision at night.</p>
<p>In one study of 90 adults, tear stability and dry eye symptoms predicted night driving trouble.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40138276/">Relationship between tear film stability, dry eye symptoms, and night driving vision among Malaysian adults</a></p>
<p>Read more on my <a href="https://courses.mentorshipu.com/learn/natural-eye-care/01-conditions/dry-eye">dry eye page</a>.</p>
<h3>Medicines</h3>
<p>Some eye drops shrink the pupil, which lets in less light.</p>
<p>In a trial of drops for reading vision, night driving was "inferior" on the drops.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38044272/">Impact of presbyopia treatment pilocarpine hydrochloride 1.25% on night-driving performance</a></p>
<p>The acne drug isotretinoin can slow adaptation to the dark.</p>
<p>One review rated "permanent loss of dark adaptation" as a probable side effect.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11530040/">Ocular side effects possibly associated with isotretinoin usage</a></p>
<p>Ask your doctor about night driving if you use drops that shrink the pupil.</p>
<p>Never stop a prescribed medicine without your doctor.</p>
<h2>Vitamin A deficiency</h2>
<p>Lack of vitamin A is a classic cause of night blindness.</p>
<p>It is common in poorer countries.</p>
<p>In 2013, about "29%" of young children in low and middle-income countries had low blood vitamin A.</p>
<p>Those children were aged 6 to 59 months.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26275329/">Trends and mortality effects of vitamin A deficiency in children in 138 low-income and middle-income countries between 1991 and 2013: a pooled analysis of population-based surveys</a></p>
<p>It also happens in wealthy countries.</p>
<p>Weight-loss surgery is one cause.</p>
<p>Among those studied for symptoms, "Night blindness prevalence was 60.1%."</p>
<p>Eye signs appeared a median of five years after gastric bypass.</p>
<p>After some other operations they took decades.</p>
<p>In 13 of 14 records with eye results, the eyes improved with treatment.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42785498/">Prevalence, Ocular Manifestations, and Treatment Response in Postbariatric Vitamin A Deficiency: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis</a></p>
<p>In children in wealthy countries, "Restrictive dietary practises were the most common mechanism."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42176959/">Xerophthalmia and ocular manifestations of vitamin A deficiency in children in high-income countries: A systematic review</a></p>
<p>Liver and gut diseases can also block vitamin A.</p>
<p>In 52 people with Crohn's disease, "Eleven (21%) had low plasma retinol."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6685675/">Vitamin A deficiency in Crohn's disease</a></p>
<p>In three people with a liver disease, vitamin A brought "full recovery of adaptation to dark."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6423181/">Vitamin A treatment for night blindness in primary biliary cirrhosis</a></p>
<p>A blood test can show whether vitamin A is low.</p>
<p>High-dose vitamin A has real risks, so take it only with your doctor.</p>
<p>Never take high-dose vitamin A if you are pregnant or could become pregnant.</p>
<h2>What supplement trials show</h2>
<h3>Vitamin A</h3>
<p>In Nepal, pregnant women given vitamin A in a supervised trial had better dark adaptation than those on placebo.</p>
<p>Their dark adaptation was worse than that of American women, and it tracked blood vitamin A.</p>
<p>If you are pregnant, take vitamin A supplements only on your doctor's advice.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11010944/">Responsiveness of dark-adaptation threshold to vitamin A and beta-carotene supplementation in pregnant and lactating women in Nepal</a></p>
<p>In 104 adults over 50, a month of high-dose vitamin A sped one measure of dark adaptation.</p>
<p>Most other measures "did not differ."</p>
<p>It made no difference to reported night driving.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16565362/">Effect of short-term, high-dose retinol on dark adaptation in aging and early age-related maculopathy</a></p>
<h3>Zinc</h3>
<p>In a Nepal trial, zinc alone "failed to restore night vision or to improve dark adaptation."</p>
<p>In night-blind pregnant women low in zinc, zinc with vitamin A may have helped.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11382658/">Zinc supplementation might potentiate the effect of vitamin A in restoring night vision in pregnant Nepalese women</a></p>
<h3>Bilberry</h3>
<p>Bilberry is often sold for night vision.</p>
<p>A review of 12 placebo-controlled trials found the four most recent randomised trials "were negative in outcome."</p>
<p>One other randomised trial and seven non-randomised trials reported benefit.</p>
<p>It found that bilberry improving normal night vision "is not supported by evidence from rigorous clinical studies."</p>
<p>Most trials tested people with healthy eyes.</p>
<p>No rigorous trial has tested bilberry in people with eye disease.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14711439/">Anthocyanosides of Vaccinium myrtillus (bilberry) for night vision--a systematic review of placebo-controlled trials</a></p>
<h3>Lutein and zeaxanthin</h3>
<p>These pigments from leafy greens have been tested in small trials.</p>
<p>In a trial of 33 older adults, glare recovery improved by 2.5 to 2.8 seconds in those supplemented.</p>
<p>Only nine people took the placebo.</p>
<p>The authors called it a "small sample RCT."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34579067/">Night Vision and Carotenoids (NVC): A Randomized Placebo Controlled Clinical Trial on Effects of Carotenoid Supplementation on Night Vision in Older Adults</a></p>
<h2>Night driving</h2>
<p>Glare from oncoming headlights is hard for older drivers.</p>
<p>In a closed-road study, "pedestrian detection decreased by 38%" with glare.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28564700/">Nighttime Driving in Older Adults: Effects of Glare and Association With Mesopic Visual Function</a></p>
<p>Yellow-tinted night driving glasses are widely sold.</p>
<p>In a simulator study, they "did not appear to improve pedestrian detection at night."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31369054/">Comparison of Pedestrian Detection With and Without Yellow-Lens Glasses During Simulated Night Driving With and Without Headlight Glare</a></p>
<p>Keep your windshield and glasses clean, inside and out.</p>
<p>If night driving feels unsafe, drive only by day.</p>
<p>Resume night driving once your eye doctor says your vision is safe for it.</p>
<h2>When to get care quickly</h2>
<p>Night vision that worsens over weeks needs an eye exam soon.</p>
<p>So does poor night vision with dry and rough eyes.</p>
<p>That can be a sign of vitamin A deficiency.</p>
<p>A new white spot on the clear front of the eye needs care the same day.</p>
<p>It can mean an infection, especially with pain or redness.</p>
<p>Any sudden loss of sight needs emergency eye care at once.</p>
<p>Halos with eye pain or nausea need emergency eye care at once.</p>
<p>So do new flashes, floaters or a shadow across your sight.</p>
<h2>How I think about night vision</h2>
<p>What follows is my own view, from many years in natural eye care.</p>
<p>It is not proven by the studies above.</p>
<p>I believe poor night vision often starts with a retina that processes light less efficiently.</p>
<p>I see it as one of the earliest signs of strain on the macula.</p>
<p>The research on lab-measured dark adaptation and macular degeneration is consistent with that view.</p>
<p>I believe circulation to the retina matters a great deal, because the macula uses so much energy.</p>
<p>I also believe the liver and gut matter, because they handle vitamin A.</p>
<p>At night I suggest red light in place of white light, to help keep night vision.</p>
<p>So the approaches I teach aim at a few things.</p>
<ul>
<li>Circulation and oxygen to the retina.</li>
<li>Good absorption of nutrients, including vitamin A.</li>
<li>Protecting the eyes from glare and excess light.</li>
<li>Regular eye exams, so a cause is not missed.</li>
</ul>
<p>Apart from correcting a proven vitamin A deficiency, none has been shown to restore night vision.</p>
<p>Use them alongside your eye doctor's care, never instead of it.</p>
<h2>What to ask your eye doctor</h2>
<p>Ask what is causing your poor night vision.</p>
<p>Ask whether you need a different prescription for night driving.</p>
<p>Ask whether cataract, the macula or your medicines play a part.</p>
<p>If you have had weight-loss surgery or gut disease, ask about a vitamin A blood test.</p>
<p>Keep working with your own eye doctor throughout.</p>
