title: "Macular degeneration: what it is, and what the research shows"

<p>By Dr. Glen Swartwout, Naturopathic Optometrist</p>
<p>I am retired from licensed practice, and I now write and teach about natural eye care.</p>
<p>This page is education, not a diagnosis or a treatment plan.</p>
<p>Macular degeneration damages the macula, the part of the retina used for reading and faces.</p>
<p>Side vision usually stays.</p>
<p>One review calls it "the leading cause of severe and irreversible vision loss in the Western world."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16151432/">Smoking and age-related macular degeneration: a review of association</a></p>
<p>A 2014 review estimated it affected 196 million people by 2020, "increasing to 288 million in 2040."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25104651/">Global prevalence of age-related macular degeneration and disease burden projection for 2020 and 2040: a systematic review and meta-analysis</a></p>
<h2>Early, dry and wet</h2>
<p>Early and intermediate stages show drusen, the yellow deposits under the retina.</p>
<p>My page on drusen explains what they are, and what the research shows about their change over time.</p>
<p>The late stage takes one of two forms.</p>
<p>Late dry disease is called geographic atrophy.</p>
<p>Patches of retina slowly waste away.</p>
<p>In wet disease, new blood vessels grow under the retina and leak.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23332590/">Clinical classification of age-related macular degeneration</a></p>
<p>Wet disease can harm sight quickly.</p>
<p>Sudden blurring or wavy lines need an eye doctor within days.</p>
<h2>Who is at risk</h2>
<p>Age is the largest risk factor.</p>
<p>Genes matter too.</p>
<p>One large study found 52 gene variants linked with the disease, across 34 regions of the genome.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26691988/">A large genome-wide association study of age-related macular degeneration highlights contributions of rare and common variants</a></p>
<p>Smoking is the largest risk you can change.</p>
<p>One review of 17 studies found "increased risk of AMD of two- to three-fold in current-smokers."</p>
<p>Five of those studies found no link.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16151432/">Smoking and age-related macular degeneration: a review of association</a></p>
<p>In another study, risk rose with the number of years smoked.</p>
<p>There, "the risk in those who had not smoked for over 20 years was comparable to non-smokers."</p>
<p>That study also linked passive smoking with higher risk.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16361672/">Smoking and age related macular degeneration: the number of pack years of cigarette smoking is a major determinant of risk for both geographic atrophy and choroidal neovascularisation</a></p>
<p>These smoking studies observed people.</p>
<p>None was a trial.</p>
<h2>What food studies show</h2>
<p>One study followed about 100000 people for two decades.</p>
<p>People with the highest predicted blood levels of lutein and zeaxanthin had about 40% less advanced disease.</p>
<p>The study found no link with intermediate disease.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26447482/">Intakes of Lutein, Zeaxanthin, and Other Carotenoids and Age-Related Macular Degeneration During 2 Decades of Prospective Follow-up</a></p>
<p>Lutein and zeaxanthin are found in leafy greens such as kale and spinach, and in egg yolk.</p>
<p>Eating fish at least twice a week was linked with less late disease.</p>
<p>The same review found "insufficient evidence" to recommend fish for prevention.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18541848/">Dietary omega-3 fatty acid and fish intake in the primary prevention of age-related macular degeneration: a systematic review and meta-analysis</a></p>
<p>Studies like these show links.</p>
<p>They cannot prove that the food caused the difference.</p>
<h2>The large supplement trials</h2>
<p>The first large trial included 3640 people.</p>
<p>It tested antioxidants and zinc, alone or together.</p>
<p>The antioxidants were vitamin C, vitamin E and beta carotene.</p>
<p>Copper was added alongside the zinc.</p>
<p>The full combination lowered the odds of advanced disease, with an odds ratio of 0.72.</p>
<p>That is roughly a quarter lower.</p>
<p>The benefit was in people at higher risk, not in those with early disease.</p>
<p>People with early changes had "only a 1.3% 5-year probability of progression to advanced AMD."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11594942/">A randomized, placebo-controlled, clinical trial of high-dose supplementation with vitamins C and E, beta carotene, and zinc for age-related macular degeneration and vision loss: AREDS report no. 8</a></p>
<p>The second trial included 4203 people.</p>
<p>It added lutein and zeaxanthin or omega-3 oils to the first formula.</p>
<p>Its main result: "no statistically significant reduction in progression to advanced AMD."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23644932/">Lutein + zeaxanthin and omega-3 fatty acids for age-related macular degeneration: the Age-Related Eye Disease Study 2 (AREDS2) randomized clinical trial</a></p>
<p>Over ten years, lutein and zeaxanthin in place of beta carotene was linked with somewhat less late disease.</p>
<p>The same follow-up found "Beta carotene usage nearly doubled the risk of lung cancer."</p>
<p>The extra lung cancers were mostly in former smokers.</p>
<p>So smokers and former smokers should avoid beta carotene.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35653117/">Long-term Outcomes of Adding Lutein/Zeaxanthin and ω-3 Fatty Acids to the AREDS Supplements on Age-Related Macular Degeneration Progression: AREDS2 Report 28</a></p>
<h2>Trials that found no significant effect</h2>
<p>Omega-3 oils showed no significant benefit in that trial, or at ten years.</p>
<p>A trial of 25871 people found no significant effect of omega-3 or vitamin D on macular degeneration.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33119047/">Effect of Vitamin D and ω-3 Fatty Acid Supplementation on Risk of Age-Related Macular Degeneration: An Ancillary Study of the VITAL Randomized Clinical Trial</a></p>
<p>Vitamin E alone "does not prevent the development or progression" of the disease, one trial found.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12098721/">Vitamin E supplementation and macular degeneration: randomised controlled trial</a></p>
<p>A ten-year trial in 39876 women found vitamin E "had no large beneficial or harmful effect."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20153900/">Vitamin E and age-related macular degeneration in a randomized trial of women</a></p>
<h2>Other ingredients studied</h2>
<p>Saffron has been tested in one small trial of mild to moderate disease.</p>
<p>In that trial of 100 people, it "modestly improved visual function."</p>
<p>The average gain was 0.69 letters on the eye chart, which is less than one letter.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30343354/">Saffron therapy for the treatment of mild/moderate age-related macular degeneration: a randomised clinical trial</a></p>
<p>Over the next year with no comparison group, retinal electrical responses improved modestly.</p>
<p>Vision was 1.6 letters worse on average.</p>
<p>The authors thought cataract changes may explain it.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38485112/">Saffron therapy for the ongoing treatment of age-related macular degeneration</a></p>
<p>A trial of 106 people tested acetyl-L-carnitine, omega-3 oils and coenzyme Q10 together.</p>
<p>It reported a "Decrease in drusen-covered area of treated eyes" compared with placebo.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15947501/">Improvement of visual functions and fundus alterations in early age-related macular degeneration treated with a combination of acetyl-L-carnitine, n-3 fatty acids, and coenzyme Q10</a></p>
<p>That was one trial of three ingredients together.</p>
<p>It does not show which one mattered.</p>
<h2>Medical treatment</h2>
<p>Wet disease is treated with injections into the eye that block new vessel growth.</p>
<p>In one trial these "prevented vision loss and improved mean visual acuity."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17021318/">Ranibizumab for neovascular age-related macular degeneration</a></p>
<p>If you have wet disease, nothing on this page replaces or delays your injections.</p>
<p>Two injected drugs now treat geographic atrophy.</p>
<p>Across their trials, atrophy grew 11% to 22% more slowly than with sham treatment.</p>
<p>One 12-month result did not reach statistical significance.</p>
<p>The pegcetacoplan trials found "no differences in key secondary visual function endpoints."</p>
<p>New wet disease was more common in treated eyes.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37865470/">Pegcetacoplan for the treatment of geographic atrophy secondary to age-related macular degeneration (OAKS and DERBY), The Lancet, 2023</a></p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37696275/">Efficacy and safety of avacincaptad pegol in geographic atrophy (GATHER2): 12-month results, The Lancet, 2023</a></p>
<p>A light treatment has also been tested in intermediate dry disease.</p>
<p>Its trial found a gain of 2.4 letters over the comparison group.</p>
<p>The comparison group also gained 3 letters.</p>
<p>Some of the authors worked for the device maker.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37972955/">LIGHTSITE III: 13-Month Efficacy and Safety Evaluation of Multiwavelength Photobiomodulation in Nonexudative (Dry) Age-Related Macular Degeneration, Retina, 2024</a></p>
<h2>How I think about macular degeneration</h2>
<p>What follows is my own view, from many years in natural eye care.</p>
<p>It is not proven by the studies above.</p>
<p>I think of the early stage as a waste problem, where the eye takes in more than it can clear.</p>
<p>I believe sugar damage to proteins plays a large part.</p>
<p>I also believe the liver's handling of fats and the circulation to the eye matter.</p>
<p>So the approaches I teach aim at four things.</p>
<ul>
<li>Glycation, through diet and blood sugar balance.</li>
<li>The liver's handling of fats.</li>
<li>Circulation and oxygen to the back of the eye.</li>
<li>Good nutrition for the retina.</li>
</ul>
<p>Not smoking belongs in any approach.</p>
<p>None of these approaches has been shown in a trial to reverse macular degeneration.</p>
<h2>What to ask your eye doctor</h2>
<p>Ask which stage you have, in each eye.</p>
<p>Ask for an OCT scan, and keep copies of the reports.</p>
<p>Ask whether a supplement like those in the large trials fits your stage.</p>
<p>Check each eye alone with a grid of straight lines, and report any new waviness at once.</p>
<p>Keep working with your own eye doctor throughout.</p>
