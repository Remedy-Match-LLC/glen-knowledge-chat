title: "Posner-Schlossman syndrome: what it is, and what the research shows"

<p>By Dr. Glen Swartwout, Naturopathic Optometrist</p>
<p>I am retired from licensed practice, and I now write and teach about natural eye care.</p>
<p>This page is education, not a diagnosis or a treatment plan.</p>
<p>See an eye doctor that day if an eye blurs or you see halos around lights.</p>
<p>Pain and redness may be mild, so do not wait for them.</p>
<h2>What it is</h2>
<p>Posner-Schlossman syndrome is also called glaucomatocyclitic crisis.</p>
<p>One review calls it "a unilateral ocular condition."</p>
<p>It has "recurrent attacks of nongranulomatous anterior uveitis and raised intraocular pressure."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28012873/">Posner-Schlossman syndrome</a></p>
<p>Put simply, the front of one eye becomes inflamed and its pressure climbs.</p>
<p>The attacks come and go.</p>
<p>Between attacks the eye can seem normal.</p>
<p>The same review says it "affects predominantly middle-aged males."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28012873/">Posner-Schlossman syndrome</a></p>
<h2>A virus in the eye</h2>
<p>Most adults carry cytomegalovirus without knowing it.</p>
<p>It is called CMV for short.</p>
<p>A 2019 review estimated a "global CMV seroprevalence of 83%" in the general population.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30706584/">Estimation of the worldwide seroprevalence of cytomegalovirus: A systematic review and meta-analysis</a></p>
<p>The 2017 review says the syndrome "is most likely the result of recurrent cytomegalovirus infection."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28012873/">Posner-Schlossman syndrome</a></p>
<p>In one series, CMV was found in the eye fluid of 68 of 126 people with the syndrome.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25124264/">Clinical outcomes in cytomegalovirus-positive Posner-Schlossman syndrome patients treated with topical ganciclovir therapy</a></p>
<p>So the virus is a main suspect, but it is not found in everyone.</p>
<p>A small 1995 study tested eye fluid from three people with the syndrome.</p>
<p>All three held herpes simplex virus, and none held CMV.</p>
<p>It concluded that "Herpes simplex virus may play a role in the origin of Posner-Schlossman syndrome."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7785697/">Possible role of herpes simplex virus in the origin of Posner-Schlossman syndrome</a></p>
<h2>Inflammation and pressure</h2>
<p>A review looked at CMV inflammation in the front of the eye, a closely related condition.</p>
<p>About 95% of acute cases had high pressure.</p>
<p>The inflammation itself was usually mild.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34954093/">Clinical characteristics and treatment outcomes of cytomegalovirus anterior uveitis and endotheliitis: A systematic review and meta-analysis</a></p>
<p>Eye fluid from people with the syndrome carried higher levels of some immune signals.</p>
<p>One study found that "PSS eyes were characterized by elevated aqueous chemokine concentration."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23028541/">Aqueous cytokine changes associated with Posner-Schlossman syndrome with and without human cytomegalovirus</a></p>
<h2>How sight can be lost</h2>
<p>Over years, the syndrome can damage the optic nerve as glaucoma does.</p>
<p>A 2020 review pooled 13 studies.</p>
<p>About one in four people with the syndrome had optic nerve damage.</p>
<p>Damage "is more likely to occur in patients who are older, and have a long course of the disease."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32737728/">A meta-analysis of the rate and related factors of glaucomatous optic nerve damage in patients with Glaucomatocyclitic Crisis</a></p>
<p>A 2023 review of 19 studies found a similar rate.</p>
<p>It warned that doctors "should not be highly optimistic about the prognosis."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37652409/">The Incidence and Risk Factors of Optic Nerve Damage of Posner-Schlossman Syndrome: A Meta-Analysis</a></p>
<p>The virus can also harm the clear front window of the eye, the cornea.</p>
<p>Eyes with CMV showed "Severe endothelial cell loss" in one series.</p>
<p>Over treated follow-up, cell loss was similar with or without CMV.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25124264/">Clinical outcomes in cytomegalovirus-positive Posner-Schlossman syndrome patients treated with topical ganciclovir therapy</a></p>
<p>Learn more about optic nerve damage on the <a href="https://courses.mentorshipu.com/learn/natural-eye-care/01-conditions/glaucoma">glaucoma page</a>.</p>
<h2>Can lost sight come back?</h2>
<p>That depends on what failed.</p>
<p>A cornea damaged by the virus can sometimes be treated.</p>
<p>A review covers CMV infection of the cornea, a related condition.</p>
<p>It says untreated disease can end up "necessitating keratoplasty or glaucoma surgery."</p>
<p>Keratoplasty is a corneal transplant.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38417101/">Cytomegalovirus Corneal Endotheliitis: A Comprehensive Review</a></p>
<p>In early glaucoma, a nerve signal test sometimes improved after pressure was lowered.</p>
<p>The authors wrote that "Retinal ganglion cell function can be at least partially restored."</p>
<p>Eyes with severe field loss "showed little improvement."</p>
<p>That study measured a nerve signal rather than sight.</p>
<p>It also did not study this syndrome.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15629815/">Restoration of retinal ganglion cell function in early glaucoma after intraocular pressure reduction: a pilot study</a></p>
<p>I have found no study showing sight returning once optic nerve cells have died.</p>
<p>So if you have lost sight, ask your eye doctor which part of the eye is the cause.</p>
<p>Keep every follow-up visit while you find out.</p>
<h2>Conventional care</h2>
<p>Conventional care is the tested treatment, and it comes first.</p>
<p>Steroid drops calm an attack, and pressure-lowering drugs protect the nerve.</p>
<p>One study reports "All patients were treated with appropriate corticosteroids and intraocular pressure-lowering drugs."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35355609/">2% Ganciclovir Eye Drops Control Posner-Schlossman Syndrome Relapses With/Without Cytomegalovirus Intraocular Reactivation</a></p>
<p>Use steroid drops only as prescribed.</p>
<p>Never restart old drops on your own.</p>
<p>When CMV is found, antiviral gel or drops are used.</p>
<p>One series found ganciclovir "effective for clearing the viral load, assisting the IOP control, and preserving the corneal endothelium."</p>
<p>It added that "Early diagnosis and proper treatment could decrease the risk of advanced glaucoma."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25124264/">Clinical outcomes in cytomegalovirus-positive Posner-Schlossman syndrome patients treated with topical ganciclovir therapy</a></p>
<p>A review found that "Recurrence of inflammation was common after cessation of maintenance therapy."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34954093/">Clinical characteristics and treatment outcomes of cytomegalovirus anterior uveitis and endotheliitis: A systematic review and meta-analysis</a></p>
<p>The 2017 review notes that "surgical intervention may be required."</p>
<p>It also mentions "aqueous and blood sampling aiding the identification" of an infectious cause.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28012873/">Posner-Schlossman syndrome</a></p>
<p>Ask your eye doctor whether an eye fluid test suits you.</p>
<p>Never stop eye drops without your eye doctor.</p>
<h2>Alongside care: terrain</h2>
<p>What follows is my own view, and it is not proven.</p>
<p>In my view, a virus that sleeps in the body wakes when the body's defences dip.</p>
<p>In my teaching I look at that ground, which I call the terrain.</p>
<p>I believe sleep and stress shape it.</p>
<p>So do nutrition and toxic load.</p>
<p>In my view, the aim is a body in which the virus stays quiet.</p>
<p>I know of no study that has tested this approach in this syndrome.</p>
<p>This view adds to your eye doctor's care.</p>
<p>It never replaces antiviral, steroid or pressure treatment.</p>
<p>Nutrients studied for the optic nerve in glaucoma are covered on the <a href="https://courses.mentorshipu.com/learn/natural-eye-care/01-conditions/glaucoma">glaucoma page</a>.</p>
<p>None has been tested in this syndrome.</p>
<h2>Protect the other eye</h2>
<p>The syndrome usually affects one eye.</p>
<p>One review found that both eyes being involved might raise the risk of nerve damage.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37652409/">The Incidence and Risk Factors of Optic Nerve Damage of Posner-Schlossman Syndrome: A Meta-Analysis</a></p>
<p>An earlier review found no clear difference.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32737728/">A meta-analysis of the rate and related factors of glaucomatous optic nerve damage in patients with Glaucomatocyclitic Crisis</a></p>
<p>Damage can progress between attacks without symptoms.</p>
<p>So keep regular checks of both eyes, including the nerve and visual field.</p>
<p>See an eye doctor that day if an eye blurs or you see halos.</p>
