title: "Diabetic retinopathy: what it is, and what the research shows"
---
<p>By Dr. Glen Swartwout, Naturopathic Optometrist</p>
<p>I am retired from licensed practice, and I now write and teach about natural eye care.</p>
<p>This page is education, not a diagnosis or a treatment plan.</p>
<p>Diabetic retinopathy is damage to the small blood vessels of the retina from diabetes.</p>
<p>A large study of pooled data found it in "34.6%" of people with diabetes.</p>
<p>It estimated "approximately 93 million people with DR."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22301125/">Global prevalence and major risk factors of diabetic retinopathy</a></p>
<h2>Stages</h2>
<p>In the early stage, small vessels leak and bulge.</p>
<p>In the proliferative stage, fragile new vessels grow and can bleed.</p>
<p>Fluid can also build up in the macula, the centre of sight.</p>
<p>This is called macular edema.</p>
<p>Early stages often cause no symptoms.</p>
<p>That is why a yearly dilated eye exam matters for anyone with diabetes.</p>
<h2>When to see an eye doctor at once</h2>
<p>Sudden new floaters need care the same day.</p>
<p>So does a curtain over vision, or sudden blur.</p>
<p>These can mean bleeding inside the eye.</p>
<p>Laser treatment of the fragile new vessels "reduces the risk of severe visual loss by 50% or more."</p>
<p>Laser can also reduce side vision or sharpness in some eyes.</p>
<p>The benefit outweighed the harm in eyes with the most severe changes.</p>
<p>For milder stages, the study gave no clear choice.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7196564/">Photocoagulation treatment of proliferative diabetic retinopathy. Clinical application of Diabetic Retinopathy Study (DRS) findings, DRS Report Number 8. The Diabetic Retinopathy Study Research Group</a></p>
<h2>Who is at risk</h2>
<p>Retinopathy was more common with longer diabetes, higher A1c and higher blood pressure.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22301125/">Global prevalence and major risk factors of diabetic retinopathy</a></p>
<p>Hemoglobin A1c is a blood test of average sugar over about three months.</p>
<p>High blood fats matter too.</p>
<p>People with retinopathy and high cholesterol were "twice as likely to have retinal hard exudates."</p>
<p>Hard exudates are a type of fatty leak.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8790092/">Association of elevated serum lipid levels with retinal hard exudate in diabetic retinopathy. Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) Report 22</a></p>
<p>In type 1 diabetes, pregnancy brought "a transient increase in the risk of retinopathy."</p>
<p>The authors advised more eye checks during pregnancy and for a year after.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10937502/">Effect of pregnancy on microvascular complications in the diabetes control and complications trial. The Diabetes Control and Complications Trial Research Group</a></p>
<p>The smoking evidence is mixed.</p>
<p>In type 1 diabetes, smokers had more retinopathy.</p>
<p>In type 2 diabetes, the same review found less.</p>
<p>The authors said this "did not change the importance of smoking cessation."</p>
<p>This finding is no reason to start smoking or to keep smoking.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30128962/">Smoking and diabetic retinopathy in type 1 and type 2 diabetes, a meta-analysis, 2018</a></p>
<h2>Blood sugar control protects the eyes</h2>
<p>This step has the strongest evidence.</p>
<p>In type 1 diabetes, tight control "reduced the adjusted mean risk for the development of retinopathy by 76 percent."</p>
<p>It also "slowed the progression of retinopathy by 54 percent."</p>
<p>The main harm was severe low blood sugar, two to three times as often.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8366922/">The effect of intensive treatment of diabetes on the development and progression of long-term complications in insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus</a></p>
<p>The benefit lasted for years after the trial ended.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10666428/">Retinopathy and nephropathy in type 1 diabetes four years after a trial of intensive therapy, NEJM, 2000</a></p>
<p>In type 2 diabetes, tighter control cut small-vessel problems by 25%.</p>
<p>That count included the need for laser treatment.</p>
<p>Low blood sugar and weight gain were more common with tight control.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9742976/">UKPDS 33, intensive blood-glucose control in type 2 diabetes, The Lancet, 1998</a></p>
<p>Retinopathy can worsen briefly in the first year of tight control.</p>
<p>In one trial this happened in 13.1% of the tight-control group and 7.6% of the others.</p>
<p>The authors found "the long-term benefits of intensive insulin treatment greatly outweighed the risks."</p>
<p>The trial found "no evidence" that lowering sugar more slowly reduced this risk.</p>
<p>Some people have had high sugar for years and moderate retinopathy or worse.</p>
<p>For them, the authors suggested an eye exam before tight control starts.</p>
<p>They suggested exams every 3 months for 6 to 12 months.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9682700/">Early worsening of diabetic retinopathy in the Diabetes Control and Complications Trial</a></p>
<p>Never change diabetes medicine without the doctor who prescribes it.</p>
<h2>Blood pressure and blood fats</h2>
<p>In type 2 diabetes with high blood pressure, tight control gave a "34% reduction in risk."</p>
<p>That was the risk of retinopathy worsening.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9732337/">Tight blood pressure control and risk of macrovascular and microvascular complications in type 2 diabetes: UKPDS 38. UK Prospective Diabetes Study Group</a></p>
<p>A later trial found no extra eye benefit from pushing blood pressure lower still.</p>
<p>The same trial added fenofibrate to a statin.</p>
<p>Fenofibrate is a drug that lowers blood fats.</p>
<p>It slowed progression: "6.5% with fenofibrate" against "10.2% with placebo."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20587587/">Effects of medical therapies on retinopathy progression in type 2 diabetes</a></p>
<p>A 2024 trial confirmed fenofibrate slowed progression.</p>
<p>It found no effect on sharpness of sight or on quality of life.</p>
<p>Kidney filtration ran about 8 points lower on fenofibrate.</p>
<p>Fenofibrate is a prescription drug, so ask your own doctor about it.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38905569/">Effect of Fenofibrate on Progression of Diabetic Retinopathy</a></p>
<h2>Diet</h2>
<p>In a Spanish trial, a Mediterranean diet with extra olive oil was linked with less retinopathy.</p>
<p>The hazard ratio was "0.56" against a lower-fat diet.</p>
<p>A Mediterranean diet with extra nuts gave a ratio of 0.63.</p>
<p>That result was not statistically significant.</p>
<p>The study had few cases, and the eye result was a later analysis.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26370380/">Mediterranean Diet, Retinopathy, Nephropathy, and Microvascular Diabetes Complications: A Post Hoc Analysis of a Randomized Trial</a></p>
<p>In the same trial, people eating at least 500 mg of fish omega-3 daily had a "48% relatively reduced risk."</p>
<p>This was the risk of sight-threatening retinopathy.</p>
<p>The authors said this was "easily achievable with 2 weekly servings of oily fish."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27541690/">Dietary Marine ω-3 Fatty Acids and Incident Sight-Threatening Retinopathy in Middle-Aged and Older Individuals With Type 2 Diabetes: Prospective Investigation From the PREDIMED Trial</a></p>
<p>Studies like these show links.</p>
<p>They cannot prove that the food caused the difference.</p>
<h2>Supplement trials</h2>
<p>Omega-3 capsules did not help.</p>
<p>A trial of 7360 people tested 1 gram of omega-3 a day.</p>
<p>Its results "exclude any clinically meaningful benefits" of that dose for retinopathy.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38052385/">ASCEND-Eye: Effects of Omega-3 Fatty Acids on Diabetic Retinopathy</a></p>
<p>Fish as food and fish oil as a capsule gave different results.</p>
<p>Alpha-lipoic acid also failed in a trial of 467 people.</p>
<p>The authors concluded that 600 mg a day "does not prevent" a form of macular edema.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21811051/">Alpha-lipoic acid for the prevention of diabetic macular edema</a></p>
<p>A 12-week trial of 60 people tested curcumin with piperine.</p>
<p>It found "no significant effect" on retinal scans.</p>
<p>It did improve some blood markers.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39744187/">Curcumin-piperine in nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy, a randomized controlled trial, 2024</a></p>
<p>Benfotiamine is a form of vitamin B1.</p>
<p>It prevented retinopathy in diabetic animals.</p>
<p>I found no human trial of it for retinopathy.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12592403/">Benfotiamine blocks three major pathways of hyperglycemic damage and prevents experimental diabetic retinopathy</a></p>
<h2>Lifestyle</h2>
<p>A Finnish trial taught diet and exercise to people with prediabetes.</p>
<p>Years later, small bulges in retinal vessels were found in 24% of the lifestyle group.</p>
<p>They were found in 38% of the others.</p>
<p>These bulges are called microaneurysms.</p>
<p>They are an early sign of retinopathy.</p>
<p>These people did not yet have diabetes when they started.</p>
<p>So this result may not apply to someone who already has diabetes.</p>
<p>Eye photos were offered at four of the five clinics.</p>
<p>After poor photos were removed, 211 of the 522 people remained.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31340493/">Life Style Intervention Improves Retinopathy Status-The Finnish Diabetes Prevention Study</a></p>
<h2>Medical treatment</h2>
<p>Laser treatment remains in use for some stages.</p>
<p>For macular edema, focal laser "substantially reduces the risk of visual loss."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2866759/">Photocoagulation for diabetic macular edema. Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study report number 1. Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study research group</a></p>
<p>Injections that block new vessel growth are now a main treatment for macular edema.</p>
<p>In one trial, average gains ranged from about 8 to 19 letters on the eye chart.</p>
<p>The gain depended on how poor vision was at the start.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25692915/">Aflibercept, bevacizumab, or ranibizumab for diabetic macular edema</a></p>
<p>If you have been offered treatment, nothing on this page replaces or delays it.</p>
<h2>How I think about diabetic retinopathy</h2>
<p>What follows is my own view, from many years in natural eye care.</p>
<p>It is not proven by the studies above.</p>
<p>I believe sugar damage to proteins drives much of the harm.</p>
<p>This damage is called glycation.</p>
<p>There is some support for this.</p>
<p>In one study, skin markers of glycation predicted retinopathy worsening over ten years.</p>
<p>Once those markers were counted, "The predictive effect of A1C vanished."</p>
<p>That was one study of 211 people with type 1 diabetes.</p>
<p>The authors wrote that glycation "may explain" this risk.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16249432/">Glycation and carboxymethyllysine levels in skin collagen predict the risk of future 10-year progression of diabetic retinopathy and nephropathy in the diabetes control and complications trial and epidemiology of diabetes interventions and complications participants with type 1 diabetes</a></p>
<p>So the approaches I teach aim at a few things.</p>
<ul>
<li>Steady blood sugar, through diet and activity.</li>
<li>Less glycation, through food choices and cooking methods.</li>
<li>Healthy circulation and blood pressure.</li>
<li>Good nutrition for the retina, including oily fish.</li>
</ul>
<p>No study above tested food choices or cooking methods for glycation.</p>
<p>None of these approaches has been shown in a trial to reverse diabetic retinopathy.</p>
<p>Use them alongside your diabetes care, never instead of it.</p>
<h2>What to ask your eye doctor</h2>
<p>Ask for a dilated eye exam every year, or as often as advised.</p>
<p>Ask which stage you have, in each eye.</p>
<p>Ask whether you have macular edema.</p>
<p>Tell your eye doctor before a big change in diabetes treatment.</p>
<p>Keep working with your own eye doctor and diabetes doctor throughout.</p>
