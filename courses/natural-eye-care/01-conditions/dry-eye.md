title: "Dry eye: what it is, and what the research shows"
---
<p>By Dr. Glen Swartwout, Naturopathic Optometrist</p>
<p>I am retired from licensed practice, and I now write and teach about natural eye care.</p>
<p>This page is education, not a diagnosis or a treatment plan.</p>
<p>Dry eye is a problem with the tear film that coats the front of the eye.</p>
<p>Eyes can feel gritty or burning.</p>
<p>They can also feel tired or watery.</p>
<p>Vision can blur and then clear with a blink.</p>
<p>An international expert report puts the cause this way.</p>
<p>It says "loss of homeostasis of the tear film is the central pathophysiological concept."</p>
<p>In plain words, the tears lose their healthy balance.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28736335/">TFOS DEWS II Definition and Classification Report</a></p>
<p>Dry eye is common.</p>
<p>A review of studies worldwide found "The prevalence of DED ranged from 5 to 50%."</p>
<p>DED stands for dry eye disease.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28736337/">TFOS DEWS II Epidemiology Report</a></p>
<h2>Two main types</h2>
<p>In one type, the eye makes too little watery tear fluid.</p>
<p>In the other, tears dry off too fast.</p>
<p>That second type often comes from blocked oil glands in the eyelids.</p>
<p>These are called meibomian glands.</p>
<p>Many people have both types.</p>
<p>The expert report says the two types "exist as a continuum."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28736335/">TFOS DEWS II Definition and Classification Report</a></p>
<h2>Who is at risk</h2>
<p>Dry eye becomes more common with age.</p>
<p>In one American study, it rose from "8.4% in subjects younger than 60 years" to 19% in those over 80.</p>
<p>Women had more dry eye than men.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10980773/">Prevalence of and risk factors for dry eye syndrome</a></p>
<p>A Dutch study of 79866 people found dry eye in 9.1%.</p>
<p>In this study, symptoms were most common in people aged 20 to 30.</p>
<p>It found "strong associations" with female sex and contact lens use.</p>
<p>Eye surgery and connective tissue diseases were also linked.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32376389/">Prevalence and risk factors of dry eye in 79,866 participants of the population-based Lifelines cohort study in the Netherlands</a></p>
<p>A review found LASIK surgery reduced tear production afterwards.</p>
<p>For tear production, other laser surgeries showed no significant change.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31712000/">Dry eye after refractive surgery: a meta-analysis</a></p>
<p>Some medicines are linked with dry eye.</p>
<p>One long study named antihistamines and drugs for anxiety or depression.</p>
<p>It also named steroid pills.</p>
<p>In a fuller analysis, only antidepressants and steroid pills stayed linked.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18677233/">Long-term incidence of dry eye in an older population</a></p>
<p>The acne drug isotretinoin is one example.</p>
<p>A 2026 review said dry eye "appears to be a common and potentially serious side effect."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42554012/">Isotretinoin and Dry Eye Disease: A Systematic Review</a></p>
<p>Never stop a prescribed medicine without your doctor.</p>
<p>Screens matter too.</p>
<p>We blink less often and less fully while we look at a screen.</p>
<p>A review of screen workers estimated dry eye in about half of them.</p>
<p>The authors warned that the estimate "lacked reliability" because the studies differed so much.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26769784/">Prevalence of dry eye disease in visual display terminal workers: a systematic review and meta-analysis</a></p>
<p>Dry air is another factor.</p>
<p>In a test chamber, very low humidity made tears less stable and eyes less comfortable.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23023409/">The effect of low humidity on the human tear film</a></p>
<h2>When to get care quickly</h2>
<p>Dry eye itself is not an emergency.</p>
<p>Some red, sore eyes are.</p>
<p>Severe eye pain or sudden loss of vision needs emergency eye care at once.</p>
<p>Contact lens wearers with a painful red eye should take the lens out and get care that day.</p>
<p>Do not put the lens back in until the eye is checked.</p>
<p>A painful red eye in a lens wearer can mean an infection of the cornea.</p>
<p>Sleeping in lenses carries more risk.</p>
<p>One study found "Overnight use of any CL is associated with a higher risk than daily use."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18538404/">The incidence of contact lens-related microbial keratitis in Australia</a></p>
<h2>Omega-3 oils</h2>
<p>The results on omega-3 go both ways.</p>
<p>A year-long trial in moderate to severe dry eye gave 3000 mg a day or an olive oil placebo.</p>
<p>The omega-3 group "did not have significantly better outcomes" than the placebo group.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29652551/">n-3 Fatty Acid Supplementation for the Treatment of Dry Eye Disease</a></p>
<p>A trial of 23523 healthy older adults tested whether 1 gram a day prevents dry eye.</p>
<p>Its hazard ratio was 0.97, which means no clear effect.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35679030/">Efficacy of Marine ω-3 Fatty Acid Supplementation vs Placebo in Reducing Incidence of Dry Eye Disease in Healthy US Adults: A Randomized Clinical Trial</a></p>
<p>A Cochrane review of 34 trials called the evidence "uncertain and inconsistent."</p>
<p>Against placebo, there "may be little to no reduction in dry eye symptoms."</p>
<p>It found a probable gain in tear production.</p>
<p>The authors did not judge this gain "to be clinically meaningful."</p>
<p>It saw "a possible role" for omega-3.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31847055/">Omega-3 and omega-6 polyunsaturated fatty acids for dry eye disease</a></p>
<p>A 2023 review of 19 trials found omega-3 did help.</p>
<p>Its authors added that "caution is needed in generalizing" the result.</p>
<p>High doses of fish oil can affect bleeding.</p>
<p>Ask your doctor first if you take a blood thinner.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38002640/">Efficacy of Omega-3 Intake in Managing Dry Eye Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials</a></p>
<h2>Other nutrition</h2>
<p>People with dry eye tend to have lower vitamin D levels.</p>
<p>The gap in one review was "3.99 ng/ml" against healthy people.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32421222/">Vitamin D deficiency is associated with dry eye syndrome: a systematic review and meta-analysis</a></p>
<p>Studies giving vitamin D found symptoms improved.</p>
<p>The review compared people before and after taking vitamin D.</p>
<p>It did not measure the gain against a placebo.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39025755/">The efficacy of vitamin D supplementation in dry eye disease: A systematic review and meta-analysis</a></p>
<p>Vitamin A helps make the mucus layer of the tears.</p>
<p>A small three-day study found vitamin A improved "the quality, but not quantity" of tears.</p>
<p>The study had 30 young men and no placebo group.</p>
<p>One tear measure improved and another got worse.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31040640/">Short-term oral vitamin A and the tear film in dry eye, Clinical Ophthalmology, 2019</a></p>
<p>A 2026 review of vitamin trials for dry eye found "none were judged to be of high quality."</p>
<p>High-dose vitamin A can be harmful, especially in pregnancy.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41984344/">The Efficacy of Oral Vitamin Supplementation in Dry Eye Disease: A Systematic Review</a></p>
<p>Two large Dutch studies looked at diet and water intake.</p>
<p>Neither found a link with less dry eye.</p>
<p>Both found slightly more dry eye in those who ate this way or drank more.</p>
<p>A Mediterranean diet was linked with lower levels of an inflammation marker.</p>
<p>The authors said it "does not appear to be associated with lower odds of having DED."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37783428/">Greater adherence to a mediterranean diet is associated with lower C-reactive protein (CRP) levels, but not to lower odds of having dry eye disease</a></p>
<p>The water study "found no evidence that greater water intake is beneficial for DED."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35941821/">The relationship between habitual water intake and dry eye disease</a></p>
<p>Studies like these show links.</p>
<p>They cannot prove that a food or drink caused the difference.</p>
<h2>Self-care</h2>
<p>Cleaning the eyelid edges is a common first step.</p>
<p>A Cochrane review studied eyelid inflammation, called blepharitis.</p>
<p>It found "Lid hygiene may provide symptomatic relief."</p>
<p>It found no strong evidence that any treatment cured it.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22592706/">Interventions for chronic blepharitis</a></p>
<p>Warm compresses are widely used to soften blocked oil glands.</p>
<p>In one small trial, a heated eyelid mask did not beat usual care.</p>
<p>Symptoms did improve within the mask group itself.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30341026/">An Eyelid Warming Device for the Management of Meibomian Gland Dysfunction</a></p>
<p>Blinking exercises have been tested in small studies.</p>
<p>In one study without a control group, 41 people finished.</p>
<p>Their symptom scores fell from 36 to 22.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32409236/">Therapeutic benefits of blinking exercises in dry eye disease</a></p>
<p>In another, the gains "mostly returned to baseline levels two weeks after finishing."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40467388/">Optimisation of blinking exercises for dry eye disease</a></p>
<p>A desk humidifier helped computer users in a one-hour trial.</p>
<p>Tears stayed stable for "+4.0 seconds" longer.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29035923/">Randomized Trial of Desktop Humidifier for Dry Eye Relief in Computer Users</a></p>
<p>The 20-20-20 rule means a 20-second break every 20 minutes, looking 20 feet away.</p>
<p>In one small study, "Dry eye symptoms and DES decreased."</p>
<p>DES stands for digital eye strain.</p>
<p>The benefit was "not maintained one week after discontinuation."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35963776/">The effects of breaks on digital eye strain, dry eye and binocular vision: Testing the 20-20-20 rule</a></p>
<h2>Medical treatment</h2>
<p>Artificial tears are the usual first treatment.</p>
<p>A Cochrane review found they "may be generally safe, but not without adverse events."</p>
<p>It rated the quality of evidence "as low."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26905373/">Over the counter (OTC) artificial tear drops for dry eye syndrome</a></p>
<p>Cyclosporine drops calm inflammation on the eye surface.</p>
<p>A 2026 Cochrane review of 58 trials found cyclosporine may give a small benefit.</p>
<p>It rated this "low-certainty evidence."</p>
<p>Users were probably more likely to stop because of side effects.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42464966/">Topical cyclosporine A therapy for dry eye disease</a></p>
<p>Lifitegrast is another anti-inflammatory drop.</p>
<p>In a trial of 711 people, it eased eye dryness more than placebo.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28079022/">Lifitegrast for the Treatment of Dry Eye Disease: Results of a Phase III, Randomized, Double-Masked, Placebo-Controlled Trial (OPUS-3)</a></p>
<p>A taste change and irritation on use were its common side effects.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36078948/">Lifitegrast Ophthalmic Solution 5% Is a Safe and Efficient Eyedrop for Dry Eye Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis</a></p>
<p>Tiny plugs can be placed in the tear drains to keep tears on the eye.</p>
<p>A Cochrane review called the results "inconclusive."</p>
<p>Side effects included watering, itching and plugs falling out.</p>
<p>Less often, the tear sac became inflamed.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28649802/">Punctal occlusion for dry eye syndrome</a></p>
<p>Intense pulsed light treats the eyelid oil glands with flashes of light.</p>
<p>A Cochrane review of three trials found the evidence uncertain.</p>
<p>Side effects included "mild pain and burning, and the potential for partially losing eyelashes."</p>
<p>The eyelash loss was put down to operator error.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32182637/">Intense pulsed light (IPL) therapy for the treatment of meibomian gland dysfunction</a></p>
<p>Thermal pulsation heats and presses the eyelid glands in one office treatment.</p>
<p>In four trials, it eased symptoms more than warm compresses.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30844843/">Efficacy of Vectored Thermal Pulsation and Warm Compress Treatments in Meibomian Gland Dysfunction: A Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials</a></p>
<h2>How I think about dry eye</h2>
<p>What follows is my own view, from many years in natural eye care.</p>
<p>It is not proven by the studies above.</p>
<p>I see dry eyes as one sign of a whole-body pattern.</p>
<p>In my experience, dry skin and dry joints often come with it.</p>
<p>So do brittle nails and poor digestion.</p>
<p>I believe blood sugar balance plays a large part.</p>
<p>One large study found no clear link with blood sugar levels.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32376389/">Prevalence and risk factors of dry eye in 79,866 participants of the population-based Lifelines cohort study in the Netherlands</a></p>
<p>An American study did find more dry eye in people with diabetes.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10980773/">Prevalence of and risk factors for dry eye syndrome</a></p>
<p>I believe eye focusing and teamwork during screen work also matter.</p>
<p>So the approaches I teach aim at a few things.</p>
<ul>
<li>Looking for underlying causes as well as easing the symptom.</li>
<li>Steady blood sugar, through diet and activity.</li>
<li>Good nutrition for each layer of the tears.</li>
<li>Healthy screen habits, with full blinks and regular breaks.</li>
</ul>
<p>Each person's healing is different, and it can change from month to month.</p>
<p>None of these approaches has been shown in a trial to cure dry eye.</p>
<p>Use them alongside your eye doctor's care, never instead of it.</p>
<h2>What to ask your eye doctor</h2>
<p>Ask which type of dry eye you have.</p>
<p>Ask whether your eyelid oil glands are blocked.</p>
<p>Ask whether any of your medicines could be adding to it.</p>
<p>If your eyes are sore and red, get them checked soon.</p>
<p>Keep working with your own eye doctor throughout.</p>
