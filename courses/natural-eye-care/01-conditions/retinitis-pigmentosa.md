title: "Retinitis pigmentosa: what it is, and what the research shows"
---
<p>By Dr. Glen Swartwout, Naturopathic Optometrist</p>
<p>I am retired from licensed practice, and I now write and teach about natural eye care.</p>
<p>This page is education, not a diagnosis or a treatment plan.</p>
<p>Retinitis pigmentosa is a group of inherited diseases of the retina.</p>
<p>The light-sensing cells slowly stop working and die.</p>
<p>A 2018 review gives "a worldwide prevalence of 1:4000."</p>
<p>It says "more than 80 genes have been associated" with the common form.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29597005/">Non-syndromic retinitis pigmentosa</a></p>
<h2>How it usually progresses</h2>
<p>Night vision usually goes first.</p>
<p>Side vision narrows next, which is often called tunnel vision.</p>
<p>Central vision can be lost later in life.</p>
<p>A 2006 review describes loss of "side vision in young adulthood, and central vision in later life."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17113430/">Retinitis pigmentosa</a></p>
<p>The pace differs a great deal from one person to the next.</p>
<h2>Testing and monitoring</h2>
<p>An electroretinogram measures the electrical response of the retina to light.</p>
<p>It can show change "many years before" night blindness is noticed.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17113430/">Retinitis pigmentosa</a></p>
<p>Visual field tests map how far side vision reaches.</p>
<p>OCT scans measure a layer of the light-sensing cells called the ellipsoid zone.</p>
<p>In one form of the disease, that layer narrowed about 7% a year.</p>
<p>Its test-to-test variation was smaller than a year's change, unlike field tests.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23828615/">Ellipsoid zone loss in X-linked retinitis pigmentosa on OCT, JAMA Ophthalmology, 2013</a></p>
<p>Genetic testing can often find the gene involved.</p>
<p>In 1000 families with inherited retinal disease, a cause was found in "760 families (76%)."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28559085/">Clinically Focused Molecular Investigation of 1000 Consecutive Families with Inherited Retinal Disease</a></p>
<p>Knowing the gene matters, because some treatments and trials fit only certain genes.</p>
<h2>Problems that can be treated</h2>
<p>Fluid can build up in the macula, the centre of sight.</p>
<p>This is called cystoid macular edema.</p>
<p>In one small study, eye drops that lower fluid reduced retinal swelling in 13 of 15 people.</p>
<p>There was no comparison group.</p>
<p>The swelling came back in some people who kept using the drops.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16546110/">Dorzolamide drops for macular edema in retinitis pigmentosa, American Journal of Ophthalmology, 2006</a></p>
<p>In a placebo-controlled trial of a similar tablet, swelling improved in "9 of 17 subjects."</p>
<p>Sharpness of sight improved in only a few.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8152764/">Methazolamide for macular edema in retinitis pigmentosa, Ophthalmology, 1994</a></p>
<p>Cataract is common in retinitis pigmentosa, often at a younger age.</p>
<p>Cataract surgery can help.</p>
<p>Of 113389 eyes in one database, 72 had the disease.</p>
<p>Those 72 eyes gained less sight than the others.</p>
<p>Swelling of the macula after surgery was more common, at "6.9% of RP eyes."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38311153/">Cataract Surgery Outcomes in Retinitis Pigmentosa A Comparative Clinical Database Study</a></p>
<h2>Vitamin A</h2>
<p>A trial of 601 adults tested vitamin A and vitamin E for four to six years.</p>
<p>Those taking 15000 IU of vitamin A a day had a slower decline in retinal response.</p>
<p>That measure was the electroretinogram, which is separate from useful sight.</p>
<p>In 354 people with stronger starting responses, they were "32% less likely" to lose half of it in a year.</p>
<p>Those taking 400 IU of vitamin E a day were "42% more likely to have a decline."</p>
<p>This was a supplement dose.</p>
<p>The finding says nothing about vitamin E from food.</p>
<p>Sharpness of sight "declined about 1 letter per year in all groups."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8512476/">A randomized trial of vitamin A and vitamin E supplementation for retinitis pigmentosa</a></p>
<p>A 2020 Cochrane review was more cautious.</p>
<p>It said "it is uncertain if there is a benefit of treatment with vitamin A or DHA, or both."</p>
<p>It rated much of the evidence as very low certainty.</p>
<p>It found no study showing that vitamins slowed the loss of sharp sight.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32573764/">Vitamin A and fish oils for preventing the progression of retinitis pigmentosa</a></p>
<p>High-dose vitamin A has real risks.</p>
<p>Cirrhosis of the liver has been reported with 25000 IU a day taken for six years.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2019375/">Liver damage caused by therapeutic vitamin A administration: estimate of dose-related toxicity in 41 cases</a></p>
<p>In pregnancy, birth defects rose above about 10000 IU a day of supplemental vitamin A.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7477116/">Teratogenicity of high vitamin A intake</a></p>
<p>Vitamin A is also advised against in a different eye disease, Stargardt disease.</p>
<p>That advice rests mostly on theory.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29939824/">Vitamin A in Stargardt disease-an evidence-based update</a></p>
<p>The trial dose of 15000 IU is above the pregnancy level of about 10000 IU.</p>
<p>The vitamin A trial included only adults.</p>
<p>Do not take high-dose vitamin A without your doctor and blood tests.</p>
<p>Never take high-dose vitamin A if you are pregnant or could become pregnant.</p>
<h2>Omega-3 and lutein</h2>
<p>DHA is an omega-3 fat found in fish.</p>
<p>A trial added DHA for 221 people already taking vitamin A.</p>
<p>DHA "did not, on average, slow the course of disease."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15364708/">DHA in retinitis pigmentosa with vitamin A treatment, Archives of Ophthalmology, 2004</a></p>
<p>A four-year trial in one inherited form also found DHA "was not effective."</p>
<p>The authors said dropouts and few disease events limited the trial.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24805262/">Four-year placebo-controlled trial of docosahexaenoic acid in X-linked retinitis pigmentosa (DHAX trial): a randomized clinical trial</a></p>
<p>In people taking vitamin A, eating more fish omega-3 was linked with slower loss of sharp sight.</p>
<p>That finding came from a diet survey, which cannot prove cause.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22332205/">Omega-3 intake and visual acuity in retinitis pigmentosa with vitamin A, Archives of Ophthalmology, 2012</a></p>
<p>A trial of lutein in 225 nonsmokers taking vitamin A found no difference on its main field test.</p>
<p>A second field test showed slower loss of mid-side vision, at the edge of significance.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20385935/">Lutein in retinitis pigmentosa with vitamin A, Archives of Ophthalmology, 2010</a></p>
<h2>N-acetylcysteine</h2>
<p>N-acetylcysteine is an antioxidant.</p>
<p>In an early trial of 30 people with no placebo group, sharp sight improved slightly.</p>
<p>The ellipsoid zone did not change.</p>
<p>Nine stomach and bowel side effects were linked to the drug.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31805012/">N-acetylcysteine and cone function in retinitis pigmentosa, a phase 1 trial, Journal of Clinical Investigation, 2020</a></p>
<p>The authors said a larger placebo-controlled trial is needed.</p>
<p>As of October 2026, I found no published results from one.</p>
<h2>Gene therapy</h2>
<p>One gene therapy is approved for disease caused by faults in both copies of the RPE65 gene.</p>
<p>It needs enough living retinal cells to work on.</p>
<p>It is given by surgery under the retina.</p>
<p>In its main trial, "13 (65%) of 20" treated people passed a mobility test in the dimmest light.</p>
<p>No one in the comparison group did.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28712537/">RPE65 gene therapy with voretigene neparvovec, a randomised phase 3 trial, The Lancet, 2017</a></p>
<p>The gains were "maintained up to 3 to 4 years."</p>
<p>One treated person had a retinal detachment about four years later.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33798654/">Durability of Voretigene Neparvovec for Biallelic RPE65-Mediated Inherited Retinal Disease: Phase 3 Results at 3 and 4 Years</a></p>
<p>This gene causes only a small share of the disease.</p>
<p>A review put it at "1-3% of RP" in the Americas.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35098484/">Epidemiology of Mutations in the 65-kDa Retinal Pigment Epithelium (RPE65) Gene-Mediated Inherited Retinal Dystrophies: A Systematic Literature Review</a></p>
<p>Other gene therapies are still in trials, and some have failed.</p>
<p>In one trial for an X-linked form, "The primary microperimetry end point was not met."</p>
<p>It had fewer people than planned.</p>
<p>Ten serious eye-related side effects occurred across the two treated groups.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38423215/">Assessment of Visual Function with Cotoretigene Toliparvovec in X-Linked Retinitis Pigmentosa in the Randomized XIRIUS Phase 2/3 Study</a></p>
<h2>Implants and light-sensing therapy</h2>
<p>A retinal implant was tested in 30 people with end-stage disease and almost no light perception.</p>
<p>The trial had no comparison group.</p>
<p>After five years, 24 still had working implants.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27453256/">Five-Year Safety and Performance Results from the Argus II Retinal Prosthesis System Clinical Trial</a></p>
<p>In 2021, one blind person regained partial sight after a gene made other retinal cells sense light.</p>
<p>He wore special goggles to use it.</p>
<p>That was one person.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34031601/">Optogenetic therapy and partial recovery of vision, a case report, Nature Medicine, 2021</a></p>
<h2>Smoking, light and daily life</h2>
<p>In a study of 410 people, more smoking was linked with worse sharp sight.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33134916/">Effect of smoking on macular function and retinal structure in retinitis pigmentosa</a></p>
<p>Many people with the disease find bright light and glare hard to manage.</p>
<p>In a small study, yellow filters improved some visual function and grey filters made it worse.</p>
<p>That study did not test whether tinted lenses slow the disease.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41450864/">Tinted filters and visual function in retinitis pigmentosa, Journal of Ophthalmic and Vision Research, 2025</a></p>
<p>I found no human trial showing that shielding the eyes from light slows the disease.</p>
<p>Low vision services can help with reading, lighting and moving about safely.</p>
<p>In one study, reading speed was "The only modifiable factor" linked with quality of life.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34606034/">Vision-related quality of life with low vision in retinitis pigmentosa, Japanese Journal of Ophthalmology, 2021</a></p>
<h2>How I think about retinitis pigmentosa</h2>
<p>What follows is my own view, from many years in natural eye care.</p>
<p>It is not proven by the studies above.</p>
<p>Retinitis pigmentosa is inherited.</p>
<p>I believe the way genes are expressed matters as well as the genes.</p>
<p>Most people with the disease notice no symptoms in early life.</p>
<p>To me, that suggests a long period when the retina copes.</p>
<p>I believe good care may support it through that time.</p>
<p>Identical twins share the same genes.</p>
<p>A 2011 report described two pairs of identical twin sisters.</p>
<p>In each pair, one twin had retinitis pigmentosa.</p>
<p>The other was unaffected "both clinically and functionally."</p>
<p>Tests found none of the known gene faults they screened for.</p>
<p>The authors said a gene change arising after conception is still possible.</p>
<p>The authors said the explanation remains "elusive."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21283054/">Discordance for retinitis pigmentosa in two monozygotic twin pairs</a></p>
<p>To me, these twins suggest that more than genes can decide whether the disease appears.</p>
<p>The report did not study diet or any form of care.</p>
<p>So the approaches I teach aim at a few things.</p>
<ul>
<li>Supporting the retina's nutrition, with care over vitamin A doses.</li>
<li>Healthy circulation to the back of the eye.</li>
<li>Avoiding smoke and other strains on the retina.</li>
<li>Supporting the whole person, body and mind.</li>
</ul>
<p>None of these approaches has been shown in a trial to stop or reverse retinitis pigmentosa.</p>
<p>Use them alongside your eye doctor's care, never instead of it.</p>
<h2>What to ask your eye doctor</h2>
<p>Ask whether genetic testing could find the gene involved.</p>
<p>Ask whether any clinical trial fits your gene.</p>
<p>Ask about swelling of the macula, and whether it can be treated.</p>
<p>Ask whether high-dose vitamin A suits you, and how your blood will be checked.</p>
<p>Ask about low vision services before daily tasks get hard.</p>
<p>Keep working with your own eye doctor throughout.</p>
