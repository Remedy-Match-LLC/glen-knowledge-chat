title: "Cataract: what it is, and what the research shows"

<p>By Dr. Glen Swartwout, Naturopathic Optometrist</p>
<p>I am retired from licensed practice, and I now write and teach about natural eye care.</p>
<p>This page is education, not a diagnosis or a treatment plan.</p>
<p>A cataract is clouding of the lens inside the eye.</p>
<p>The lens sits behind the pupil and focuses light onto the retina.</p>
<p>As it clouds, vision blurs and colours fade.</p>
<p>Glare often gets worse.</p>
<p>Cataract is common worldwide.</p>
<p>A 2021 review found cataract was the leading cause of blindness in people aged 50 and over.</p>
<p>It counted "15·2 million cases" of blindness from cataract in 2020.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33275949/">Causes of blindness and vision impairment in 2020 and trends over 30 years, and prevalence of avoidable blindness in relation to VISION 2020: the Right to Sight: an analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study</a></p>
<h2>Types of cataract</h2>
<p>Nuclear cataract clouds the centre of the lens, and often turns it yellow or brown.</p>
<p>Cortical cataract starts as spokes at the edge of the lens.</p>
<p>Posterior subcapsular cataract forms at the back of the lens, and often troubles reading and glare.</p>
<p>Each type has somewhat different risk factors.</p>
<h2>Who is at risk</h2>
<p>Age is a major risk factor.</p>
<p>In one long study, age raised the risk of every type.</p>
<p>Diabetes raised the risk of cortical and posterior subcapsular cataract.</p>
<p>Smoking raised the risk of cortical cataract and of surgery.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21684602/">Risk factors associated with incident cataracts and cataract surgery in the Age-related Eye Disease Study (AREDS): AREDS report number 32</a></p>
<p>A review of 43 studies linked current smoking with more cataract.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30528895/">Risk of cataract in smokers: A meta-analysis of observational studies</a></p>
<p>Stopping smoking is linked with lower risk over time.</p>
<p>A study of 44371 Swedish men found that "Smoking cessation seems to decrease the risk of cataract extraction with time."</p>
<p>It added that "the risk persists for decades."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24385206/">Smoking cessation and the risk of cataract: a prospective cohort study of cataract extraction among men</a></p>
<p>Sunlight matters for cortical cataract.</p>
<p>In a study of watermen, more ultraviolet B exposure meant more cortical cataract.</p>
<p>The same study found "No association" between ultraviolet B and nuclear cataract.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3185661/">Effect of ultraviolet radiation on cataract formation</a></p>
<p>Sunglasses that block ultraviolet light cut that exposure.</p>
<p>So does a brimmed hat.</p>
<p>Steroid medicines are linked with cataract.</p>
<p>In people with asthma or COPD, steroid use carried "on average a doubled risk of cataract."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37899845/">Inhaled corticosteroids and cataract in asthma and COPD, a systematic review, Journal of Ophthalmology, 2023</a></p>
<p>Never stop a prescribed steroid without your doctor.</p>
<p>A review of seven studies found "no substantial overall increased risk" of cataract from alcohol.</p>
<p>The authors said this needs confirming.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25238065/">Alcohol intake and the risk of age-related cataracts: a meta-analysis of prospective cohort studies</a></p>
<h2>What food studies show</h2>
<p>A study of 324 female twins followed nuclear cataract for about nine years.</p>
<p>It found "dietary vitamin C protected against cataract progression."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27016950/">Genetic and Dietary Factors Influencing the Progression of Nuclear Cataract</a></p>
<p>A review of long studies linked higher vitamin C and lutein intake with less cataract.</p>
<p>It added that "evidence from RCTs is less clear."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30624584/">Dietary vitamin and carotenoid intake and risk of age-related cataract</a></p>
<p>In a large British study, people who ate the most fruit and vegetables had less cataract.</p>
<p>Legumes, tomatoes, apples and pears each showed a link on their own.</p>
<p>Green leafy vegetables, berries and citrus did not.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36973404/">Fruit and vegetable intake and the risk of cataract: insights from the UK Biobank study</a></p>
<p>Studies like these show links.</p>
<p>They cannot prove that the food caused the difference.</p>
<h2>Supplement trials</h2>
<p>The trials of antioxidant vitamins mostly found no effect.</p>
<p>In the large AREDS trial, vitamins C and E with beta carotene had "no apparent effect" on cataract.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11594943/">A randomized, placebo-controlled, clinical trial of high-dose supplementation with vitamins C and E and beta carotene for age-related cataract and vision loss: AREDS report no. 9</a></p>
<p>Adding lutein and zeaxanthin had "no statistically significant overall effect on rates of cataract surgery."</p>
<p>People who ate the least lutein may have benefited, in a smaller secondary analysis.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23645227/">Lutein/zeaxanthin for the treatment of age-related cataract: AREDS2 randomized trial report no. 4</a></p>
<p>These results are about cataract only.</p>
<p>They say nothing about the macular degeneration results of the same trials.</p>
<p>Do not stop an eye supplement without your eye doctor.</p>
<p>In 11545 men, vitamins E and C had "no notable beneficial or harmful effect."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21060040/">Age-related cataract in a randomized trial of vitamins E and C in men</a></p>
<p>In 39876 women, vitamin E "provides no benefit for age-related cataract or subtypes."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18067963/">Vitamin E and age-related cataract in a randomized trial of women</a></p>
<p>Another trial found vitamin E "did not reduce the incidence of or progression" of cataract.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14711717/">Vitamin E supplementation and cataract: randomized controlled trial</a></p>
<p>A review of nine trials found "no evidence" that antioxidant vitamins prevent or slow cataract.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22696344/">Antioxidant vitamin supplementation for preventing and slowing the progression of age-related cataract</a></p>
<p>One exception came from rural China, in poorly nourished people.</p>
<p>There, a multivitamin with minerals lowered nuclear cataract by 36% in people aged 65 to 74.</p>
<p>Riboflavin with niacin lowered it by 44% in the same age group.</p>
<p>It also raised posterior subcapsular cataract, though cases were few.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8363468/">The Linxian cataract studies. Two nutrition intervention trials</a></p>
<p>That result may not apply to people who are already well fed.</p>
<h2>Eye drops</h2>
<p>Drops of N-acetylcarnosine have been studied.</p>
<p>A review found "no convincing evidence that NAC reverses cataract, nor prevents progression."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28245346/">N-acetylcarnosine (NAC) drops for age-related cataract</a></p>
<p>In a 2015 study, lanosterol "could reduce cataract severity" in rabbit lenses removed from the eye.</p>
<p>It did the same in dogs.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26200341/">Lanosterol reverses protein aggregation in cataracts</a></p>
<p>A later test soaked 40 removed human cataract lenses in lanosterol.</p>
<p>One found lanosterol "did not reverse opacification of human age-related cataractous nuclei."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26862091/">Effect of lanosterol on human cataract nucleus</a></p>
<p>I found no controlled human trial of lanosterol.</p>
<h2>Surgery</h2>
<p>Surgery replaces the clouded lens with a clear artificial one.</p>
<p>A review says most people have "rapid visual recovery" and "good visual outcomes" after surgery.</p>
<p>It also reports "minimal complications" for most.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28242111/">Cataracts</a></p>
<p>Serious infection after surgery is rare.</p>
<p>A United States registry covered over 17 million eye operations.</p>
<p>After cataract surgery in adults, the rate was "1 case per 2652 procedures."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41985695/">Endophthalmitis rates across eye procedures in the IRIS Registry, Ophthalmology, 2026</a></p>
<h2>How I think about cataract</h2>
<p>What follows is my own view, from many years in natural eye care.</p>
<p>It is not proven by the studies above.</p>
<p>I see cataract as the end point of many factors beyond age.</p>
<p>I believe a yellow pigment builds up in the lens and adds to the clouding.</p>
<p>I believe toxic metals play a part, and that mercury stays longest in the lens.</p>
<p>So the approaches I teach aim at a few things.</p>
<ul>
<li>Protecting the lens from sunlight and smoke.</li>
<li>Good nutrition, including foods rich in vitamin C and lutein.</li>
<li>Blood sugar balance.</li>
<li>Reducing the load of toxic metals.</li>
</ul>
<p>Testing for metals or removing them can be done with an integrative practitioner.</p>
<p>You can find one with our <a href="https://courses.mentorshipu.com/practitioner-finder">practitioner finder</a>.</p>
<p>None of these approaches has been shown in a trial to reverse cataract.</p>
<h2>What to ask your eye doctor</h2>
<p>Ask which type of cataract you have, in each eye.</p>
<p>Ask how much it limits your vision, and whether it is changing.</p>
<p>Ask whether any of your medicines raise your risk.</p>
<p>When cataract limits daily life, ask about surgery and its risks.</p>
<p>Keep working with your own eye doctor throughout.</p>
