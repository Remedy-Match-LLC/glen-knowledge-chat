title: "Glaucoma: what it is, and what the research shows"
---
<p>By Dr. Glen Swartwout, Naturopathic Optometrist</p>
<p>I am retired from licensed practice, and I now write and teach about natural eye care.</p>
<p>This page is education, not a diagnosis or a treatment plan.</p>
<p>Glaucoma is a group of diseases that damage the optic nerve.</p>
<p>One review calls it "a group of progressive optic neuropathies characterized by degeneration of retinal ganglion cells."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24825645/">The pathophysiology and treatment of glaucoma: a review</a></p>
<p>It usually takes side vision first, and slowly.</p>
<p>Most people notice nothing until much is lost.</p>
<p>A 2021 review found "more than half of all glaucoma cases were undetected previously."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33865875/">The Global Extent of Undetected Glaucoma in Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis</a></p>
<p>About 3.5% of people aged 40 to 80 worldwide have glaucoma.</p>
<p>One review projected "111.8 million in 2040."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24974815/">Global prevalence of glaucoma and projections of glaucoma burden through 2040: a systematic review and meta-analysis</a></p>
<h2>Types of glaucoma</h2>
<p>Open-angle glaucoma is the most common type.</p>
<p>It comes on slowly and painlessly.</p>
<p>In normal-tension glaucoma, the nerve is damaged although eye pressure reads normal.</p>
<p>Angle-closure glaucoma happens when the drainage angle of the eye is blocked.</p>
<p>It can come on suddenly, or slowly with no symptoms.</p>
<h2>A medical emergency</h2>
<p>Sudden angle closure can cause "sudden onset of red eye associated with pupillary dilation, dull pain and headache."</p>
<p>The same review says "An urgent referral to the ophthalmologist is mandatory."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31822188/">Acute angle closure glaucoma</a></p>
<p>Blurred vision and halos around lights can come with it.</p>
<p>So can nausea or vomiting.</p>
<p>If you have these signs, go to an emergency room or eye doctor at once.</p>
<p>If your drainage angle is narrow, ask which medicines to avoid.</p>
<h2>Who is at risk</h2>
<p>Higher eye pressure, older age and a thin cornea all raise risk.</p>
<p>One study found that corneal thickness "was found to be a powerful predictor."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12049575/">The Ocular Hypertension Treatment Study: baseline factors that predict the onset of primary open-angle glaucoma</a></p>
<p>Family history matters.</p>
<p>In one small study, close relatives of people with glaucoma had a "risk ratio for glaucoma of 9.2."</p>
<p>The true figure may be much lower.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9869795/">Genetic risk of primary open-angle glaucoma. Population-based familial aggregation study</a></p>
<p>In Baltimore, open-angle glaucoma rates were "four to five times higher in blacks as compared with whites."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2056646/">Racial variations in the prevalence of primary open-angle glaucoma. The Baltimore Eye Survey</a></p>
<p>Nearsightedness roughly doubles the risk.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21684603/">Myopia as a risk factor for open-angle glaucoma: a systematic review and meta-analysis</a></p>
<p>Blood flow to the eye matters too.</p>
<p>In a Barbados study, low perfusion pressure "more than doubled risk."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17629563/">Risk factors for incident open-angle glaucoma: the Barbados Eye Studies</a></p>
<p>Perfusion pressure is the gap between blood pressure and eye pressure.</p>
<p>Smoking does not appear to raise glaucoma risk.</p>
<p>A 2026 review of 26 studies found an odds ratio of 1.00 for open-angle glaucoma.</p>
<p>Smoking is still a strong risk for macular degeneration and other disease.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41627566/">Tobacco smoking is not associated with primary open-angle glaucoma: a systematic review and meta-analysis</a></p>
<h2>Lowering pressure slows damage</h2>
<p>Lowering eye pressure is the treatment with the strongest proof.</p>
<p>One review says "Reduction of intraocular pressure is the only proven method to treat the disease."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24825645/">The pathophysiology and treatment of glaucoma: a review</a></p>
<p>In people with high pressure but no damage, drops cut the five-year rate of glaucoma.</p>
<p>It was "4.4% in the medication group and 9.5% in the observation group."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12049574/">The Ocular Hypertension Treatment Study: a randomized trial determines that topical ocular hypotensive medication delays or prevents the onset of primary open-angle glaucoma</a></p>
<p>In early glaucoma, lowering pressure by about a quarter meant less worsening.</p>
<p>Damage progressed in 45% of treated people and 62% of untreated people.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12365904/">Reduction of intraocular pressure and glaucoma progression: results from the Early Manifest Glaucoma Trial</a></p>
<p>A placebo-controlled trial confirmed the benefit of pressure-lowering drops.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25533656/">Latanoprost for open-angle glaucoma (UKGTS): a randomised, multicentre, placebo-controlled trial</a></p>
<p>Laser is another option.</p>
<p>Three years after laser, "74·2%" of people in one trial needed no drops.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30862377/">Selective laser trabeculoplasty versus eye drops for first-line treatment of ocular hypertension and glaucoma (LiGHT): a multicentre randomised controlled trial</a></p>
<p>Surgery is used when drops and laser are not enough.</p>
<p>Never stop glaucoma drops without your eye doctor.</p>
<h2>Nutrition and supplements</h2>
<p>Nicotinamide is a form of vitamin B3.</p>
<p>It has been tested in small trials.</p>
<p>In one, a measure of retinal function improved "14.8%" on nicotinamide and "5.2%" on placebo.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32721104/">Improvement in inner retinal function in glaucoma with nicotinamide (vitamin B3) supplementation: A crossover randomized clinical trial</a></p>
<p>A 2026 trial in normal-tension glaucoma found "No significant intergroup differences" in visual field change.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41167798/">Effects of nicotinamide supplementation in normal-tension glaucoma: a crossover placebo-controlled randomised clinical trial</a></p>
<p>A 2026 review found its "effects on visual field outcomes were inconsistent."</p>
<p>It said the evidence "remains insufficient to support routine high-dose NAM supplementation."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42739057/">Nicotinamide Supplementation in Glaucoma: A Systematic Review of Human Studies</a></p>
<p>Ginkgo biloba has mixed results in normal-tension glaucoma.</p>
<p>One small trial found it "appears to improve preexisting visual field damage."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12578781/">Effect of Ginkgo biloba extract on preexisting visual field damage in normal tension glaucoma</a></p>
<p>Another found it "had no effect on mean defect or contrast sensitivity."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24282229/">Ginkgo biloba in normal tension glaucoma, a randomized crossover trial, 2014</a></p>
<p>Ask your doctor before taking ginkgo with a blood thinner.</p>
<p>Citicoline has also been studied.</p>
<p>A 2023 review found a "lack of sufficient evidence to support that citicoline slows the progression."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37768938/">Citicoline as a supplement in glaucoma, a systematic review, 2023</a></p>
<h2>What food studies show</h2>
<p>In two large groups followed for years, people with the most dietary nitrate had less glaucoma.</p>
<p>That nitrate came "mainly from green leafy vegetables."</p>
<p>The top fifth had a rate ratio of "0.79."</p>
<p>Eating 1.45 or more servings of greens a day gave a ratio of 0.82.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26767881/">Association of Dietary Nitrate Intake With Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma: A Prospective Analysis From the Nurses' Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-up Study</a></p>
<p>Overall, caffeine "was not associated with increased risk."</p>
<p>Five or more cups of coffee a day showed a possible rise in risk.</p>
<p>Caffeine was more strongly linked with glaucoma in people with a family history.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18263806/">Caffeine consumption and the risk of primary open-angle glaucoma: a prospective cohort study</a></p>
<p>Studies like these show links.</p>
<p>They cannot prove that the food caused the difference.</p>
<h2>Exercise and posture</h2>
<p>Moderate aerobic exercise lowers eye pressure for a while.</p>
<p>A 2024 review found "transient reductions in IOP."</p>
<p>Heavy weight-lifting raised eye pressure for a while.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38722193/">The Effect of Exercise on Intraocular Pressure and Glaucoma</a></p>
<p>Head-down positions raise it.</p>
<p>In 10 people with glaucoma, downward dog raised pressure "from 17 ± 3.2 mmHg to 28 ± 3.8 mmHg."</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26698309/">Intraocular Pressure Rise in Subjects with and without Glaucoma during Four Common Yoga Positions</a></p>
<p>If you have glaucoma, ask before doing head-down poses.</p>
<h2>Feeling eye pressure by touch</h2>
<p>Before instruments, doctors estimated eye pressure by gently pressing the closed eyelid.</p>
<p>I teach this as a rough self-check.</p>
<p>It has not been tested as a self-check.</p>
<p>You alternate two fingertips gently on the closed upper lid.</p>
<p>A very high pressure can feel stony hard.</p>
<p>A normal feel does not mean your eye is healthy.</p>
<p>Do not press on an eye after recent eye surgery, injections or injury.</p>
<p>Studies of doctors using touch show it is rough.</p>
<p>One found "little correlation between tactile assessment" and instrument readings.</p>
<p>It did pick up "five of seven eyes" with very high pressure.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7733191/">Assessment of intraocular pressure by palpation</a></p>
<p>In healthy volunteers, the touch estimate was off by more than 3 points in 48% of eyes.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15722303/">Transpalpebral tonometry: reliability and comparison with Goldmann applanation tonometry and palpation in healthy volunteers</a></p>
<p>So touch can raise an alarm.</p>
<p>It cannot rule glaucoma out.</p>
<p>Only your eye doctor's instruments can measure pressure and check the nerve.</p>
<h2>How I think about glaucoma</h2>
<p>What follows is my own view, from many years in natural eye care.</p>
<p>It is not proven by the studies above.</p>

<p>My eye pressures became normal.</p>
<p>I cannot say which of these made the difference.</p>
<p>Normal pressure alone does not prove the nerve is safe.</p>
<p>That is one person's experience.</p>
<p>It is not proof that the same will happen for anyone else.</p>

<p>I believe the pressure balance between the eye and the spinal fluid at the optic nerve matters.</p>
<p>I believe pressure often swings more between day and night in glaucoma.</p>
<p>I also believe excess glutamate harms the nerve cells.</p>
<p>So the approaches I teach aim at a few things.</p>
<ul>
<li>Circulation and blood flow to the optic nerve.</li>
<li>Steady pressure through the day and night.</li>
<li>Protecting nerve cells, including through diet.</li>
<li>Exercise, and care with head-down positions.</li>
</ul>
<p>None of these approaches has been shown in a trial to replace pressure-lowering treatment.</p>
<p>Use them alongside your eye doctor's treatment, never instead of it.</p>
<h2>What to ask your eye doctor</h2>
<p>Ask what your eye pressure is, and what target suits you.</p>
<p>Ask for a visual field test and an optic nerve scan, and keep the reports.</p>
<p>Ask whether your drainage angle is narrow.</p>
<p>If glaucoma runs in your family, start checks early.</p>
<p>Keep working with your own eye doctor throughout.</p>
